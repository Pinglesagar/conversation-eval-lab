A case study / Two recorded sales calls, graded by software / September 2026

The grader marked it down for not asking questions.

It asked five.

  • A trainee financial adviser practised a sales call with an AI customer, out loud. One piece of software turned the speech into text. Another graded the transcript, the way a coach would.
  • The grader decides that a sentence is a question by looking for a question mark. The transcript it was handed had no punctuation at all, so no sentence in it could ever end in one.
  • It gave 0/4 for asking questions, on a call where 5 of 8 of the adviser’s turns were questions. A second mistake moved the other way, and the total hid both.

What this page is. An open-source test bench for AI conversations, voice and text, shown through two real recorded calls. Every number on it is recomputed from files in the repository. Nothing needs an account or an API key.

Four things this surfaced / open any one

The score sheet that shipped counted words. The compliance gate reads the record.

rubric_v1 scores mandatory_disclosure by counting six English keywords in the adviser’s speech. The cited scorecard’s gate CG-1 reads the product’s own record_disclosure ledger against the codes the jurisdiction requires. Same two traces, same view, a different basis — and a gate is counted, never averaged.

Both calls were cut short — a character cap, then a content filter — so neither miss says what the adviser would have said later. The gate grades the session as recorded, which is what a compliance gate must do.

The standard is named. The evaluator prints which regime it grades eu-retail under — the FCA register, assumption A-V2-1 — and runs that register’s cited probes beside the ledger check. The three-code list itself still comes from register.py, which cites nothing; that is now a printed limitation rather than a hidden one.

The register accepted a paraphrase the FCA rule does not.

At turn 2 of call 2 the adviser said “past performance is not a guide to future performance.” The demo register matched it and recorded past_performance. The FCA probe for COBS 4.5A.10R wants “past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results” — the words, not the meaning — and marks it missed.

Two instruments read one sentence, and only one of them is reading the rule.

Call 2 is four turns long because the model refused an unpunctuated sentence.

At adviser turn 3 the trainee model refused the customer’s question about how long the money would be tied up. Thirteen probes, recorded on the day and committed as evidence: the unpunctuated transcript — what the recogniser returns and what the model was given — was refused 5 of 6 times; the same sentence with its punctuation completed 5 of 5.

The channel defect that zeroed discovery in call 1 ended call 2. Those probe results are labelled as recorded live, not recomputable offline.

23 of 28 KPIs say not applicable, and each says why.

An uncalibrated judge. A phrase set the registry names but does not commit. A gate whose trigger never occurred. Each not-applicable KPI carries its reason, and the denominator shrinks visibly instead of silently.

4/6 points and 2/3 gates, on each call, is the whole of what the repo can decide today — and it says so.

01

How it works

Everything is judged from the record. Nothing from the agent’s own account.

Every conversation is written down as one ordered record of what happened: who spoke, which tools were called, what came back. Every score is computed from that record and nothing else, so a disputed grade can be reopened months later without re-running anything.

Conversation

A caller talks to the agent under test

Scripted or model-driven personas; text, or spoken through real synthesis and recognition and graded on what was heard. Repeated k times, because one pass is not evidence.

→

The trace

One ordered event stream

15 event kinds from 3 actors, with an injected clock. Every check below is decided on the position of an event in this stream, never on its timestamp. Swap the agent, or swap text for speech, and nothing downstream changes.

→

Grader 1 / contracts

6 contracts, plain code

Did the agent actually call the tool it promised? Free, instant, the same answer every time. Literal, so it misses paraphrase — which is its own finding, below.

Grader 2 / judges

A model marks meaning

Calibrated against hand labels first: calibrate() returns TPR, TNR and kappa, and the registry raises below 0.85 on either rate. An unmeasured judge cannot run.

Grader 3 / registers

4 regimes, 36 entries

The actual rulebooks, not a keyword list. Every entry carries a paragraph-level citation or is labelled an assumption, and a not-required kind stops one market’s rule being invented in another.

→

Gate

It can say no

Two gates. The calibration gate refuses a judge below threshold. The CI gate runs 8 stages and 17 commands offline, cheapest first, and 2 of the stages must regenerate committed artefacts byte for byte.

→

Report

Every rate with its denominator

STABLE_PASS, STABLE_FAIL or FLAKY, and FLAKY is never a pass. The committed case-study report says FAIL — 44/47 stable-pass — and the build is green because it reproduced that report exactly.

Read left to right: a conversation happens, it is written down, the writing is graded three ways, a gate decides whether the graders themselves are trustworthy, and a report states every figure with the count it came from. The trace in the middle is the whole design; the rest is what you can build once you have it.

The 15 event kinds, and the 2 reserved ones

From lab/trace/schema.py::EventKind. Actors: caller, agent, system. The two reserved kinds exist in the schema so that a duplex adapter can emit them; today they are constructed from timings a scenario hands over, and no committed trace contains either.

The questions really were still there This call carries a second, independent grade of the same unpunctuated transcript: an LLM judge rather than the rule. Is the gap just punctuation? Almost — but not entirely

Both sides were normalised by lab.voice.wer.normalise — the same function the word error rate uses, which lowercases, expands contractions, strips punctuation and converts number words to digits. The reference is each turn's spoken_form, which is what was sent to the synthesiser. Each surviving edit is then classified on its own.

  • 16turns
  • 10edits
  • 9orthographic
  • 1content

The one content difference

  • Turn 6, order 11, the customer speaking.
  • Sent “I understand what you're saying.” → heard “understand what you're saying”.
  • The recogniser dropped the word I. That is the only genuine content edit on the whole call.

The nine orthographic ones

  • mr → mister ×3
  • timeframe → time frame ×3
  • summarise → summarize ×2
  • per cent → percent ×1

Which is the point: the recogniser did its job. The transcript is faithful. The failure is entirely in what the detector demands of it.

Use it on your own agent

Write one small adapter. Everything else runs unchanged.

The bench is an instrument you point at a system, not a framework the system has to adopt. Two places to plug in, each a short piece of code, and the recordings, checks and reports then work exactly as they do here.

    customer utterance to prompt it with. `None` from either means "I am done",
    and the reason should be readable afterwards from `stop_reason` — a session
    that ended because the adviser closed the sale and one that ended because the
    turn budget ran out are different results.
    """

    def open(self) -> str | None:
        """The adviser's first turn, or None to decline the session."""
        ...

    def reply(self, customer_turn: str) -> str | None:
        """The next turn given what the customer just said, or None to stop."""
        ...

open() returns the adviser's first turn or None to decline; reply(customer_turn) returns the next turn or None to stop; stop_reason, if present, says why.

What these results do not prove.

The boundary of every claim above, stated here rather than in a footnote.

  1. The spoken calls are n = 2. A mechanism, not a rate: two calls, examined end to end. Nothing on this page reads it as a frequency, and nothing should.
  2. The 16/18 is in-sample. In-sample: the probes were written with these transcripts in view. The CLI says so on its second line. A held-out paraphrase set is the honest next step.
  3. The phrase lists are short. A correct disclosure worded as “three per cent of the sum you invest” returns missed. Recall against unseen wording is unmeasured. docs/WALKTHROUGH.md §11, docs/ADVISORY_TEST_STRATEGY.md
  4. Barge-in is constructed, not discovered. The two interruption event kinds are emitted from constructed timings only; no adapter discovers one. Status in the coverage file: reserved.
  5. One market’s language is untestable. No synthesis vendor in this stack produces Cantonese, so that market has no audio path and its row is declared untestable rather than counted as covered. docs/AUDIO_SUITE.md
  6. One labeller. Every hand label set here has one labeller with reasons recorded; inter-rater agreement is not measured.
  7. Replay is blind to a prompt change. A green gate is necessary and not sufficient when a prompt, persona or rubric has moved; the recorded tiers cannot see it. docs/GATES.md, stage 9
